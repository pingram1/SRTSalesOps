## Positioning notes

**Internal. Strip this section before sending.**

**Where these prices sit.** 2026 market data: small-business sites run $1,500–$8,000 with a freelancer and $5,000–$25,000 with an agency, median $3,000–$5,000. Real maintenance starts at $95–$149/month, with $150–$350 standard. AI automation is a fixed build of $2,500–$15,000 plus $500–$2,500/month. AI advisory retainers run $1,500–$4,000/month.

Every tier is priced at or below the boutique-agency band while delivering custom work rather than templated work. That is the competitive claim: agency capability, priced between freelancer and agency. It is defensible because it's true, not because it's cheap.

**Why Starter exists.** It absorbs price-shoppers so Essential never has to be discounted. When someone balks at $4,500, the answer is Starter — not $3,500 for Essential. Protecting Essential's price is worth more than any single Starter sale.

**Why Professional should close most deals.** It's the first tier where software does work instead of just existing, and the jump from Essential buys automation rather than more pages. Lead with the hours it removes each week, not the page count.

**Margin, and where it gets thin.** Build fees sit mid-band against the market; the monthlies are deliberately priced near the bottom of their bands, because recurring is the number clients scrutinize hardest and the one that has to survive every budget review. That makes it competitive, and it also makes the included hours matter more than they look.

Run the math before you agree to anything extra:

- **Essential, $199/mo** — 2 hours included. At $150/hr list that's $300 of time against $199, so it only works because most months use well under 2 hours. Watch clients who use the full allowance every month.
- **Professional, $895/mo** — 4 dev hours plus a review call is realistically 5 hours, an effective rate near $179/hr. Healthy, but it has no room to drift. Eight hours a month turns this into charity.
- **Elite, $2,950/mo** — 20 reserved hours is roughly $148/hr effective. Priced for the relationship and the reserved-capacity commitment, not for the hours.

If a client exceeds their included hours two months running, move them up a tier or bill the overage. Absorbing it quietly is the single most common way productized pricing collapses, and it always starts as a favor.

**When to leave this card behind.** If the client's modeled annual value is above $250,000, this card underprices the work badly. Switch to [value-based-pricing-model.md](./value-based-pricing-model.md) and price against their outcome instead. The signal is usually Elite-shaped scope combined with a large, quantifiable revenue leak.
