# Discovery Triage — Picking the Right Tier on the Call

**Internal. Start Right Tutoring.**

A 15-minute structure for a discovery call that ends with you naming a tier with confidence. Score as you go, total at the end, recommend before you hang up.

Tiers and prices are in [service-tiers.md](./service-tiers.md).

---

## How to run the call

Do not open with services. Open with their situation and let the tier reveal itself.

Five questions, scored 1 to 4. Total between 5 and 20. The total maps to a tier.

Write the score in the margin as they answer. Do not compute the total in front of them — you want to name the tier as a conclusion, not as arithmetic.

---

## The five questions

### 1. "Walk me through what you need. What made you reach out?"

Open-ended on purpose. Let them talk for two minutes before you say anything.

| Score | What you're hearing |
|---|---|
| 1 | "I need a website." Nothing behind it. |
| 2 | "I need a site that brings in customers." Lead generation is the goal. |
| 3 | "I need the site to connect to how we actually work." Integration language. |
| 4 | "We need a system built." Custom software, portals, internal tools. |

### 2. "Who touches this day to day, and what do they do with it?"

Team size is the single best proxy for complexity.

| Score | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1 | Just me |
| 2 | 2–5 people |
| 3 | 6–20 people |
| 4 | 20+, or multiple locations, or departments with different needs |

### 3. "What's the most repetitive thing someone on your team does every week?"

The automation question. Ask it exactly this way — asking "do you want automation?" gets you a shrug, asking about repetitive work gets you a specific complaint.

| Score | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1 | Nothing much, or they can't name anything |
| 2 | A few hours a week, mildly annoying |
| 3 | 10+ hours a week, clearly a bottleneck |
| 4 | It's the constraint on growth — they're hiring to keep up with it |

### 4. "What software are you already running?"

Existing stack determines integration work, which is where build hours hide.

| Score | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1 | Nothing, or just email |
| 2 | One CRM or booking tool |
| 3 | 2–4 tools that don't talk to each other |
| 4 | Complex stack, or custom/legacy systems, or a real data migration |

### 5. "If this stays broken for another year, what does it cost you?"

The value question, and the one most people skip. Many clients have never calculated it. Helping them do so on the call is itself worth the call.

| Score | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1 | They don't know and can't estimate |
| 2 | Under $25,000/year |
| 3 | $25,000–$100,000/year |
| 4 | Over $100,000/year |

---

## Scoring

| Total | Recommend | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 5–8 | **Starter** | Simple need, no team, no measurable pain. Don't oversell — take the $1,500 and the upgrade path. |
| 9–12 | **Essential** | The most common landing spot for local and service businesses. |
| 13–16 | **Professional** | Real team, real repetitive work, real integrations. Should be your most-sold tier. |
| 17–20 | **Elite** | Custom software or multi-department automation. Scope properly before quoting. |

### Two overrides

**Question 5 scored 4 and they can quantify it.** If modeled annual value looks north of $250,000, stop using the rate card. Book a second call, model the value properly, and price against their outcome using [value-based-pricing-model.md](./value-based-pricing-model.md). Elite at $65,400 year one would badly underprice a client with a $400,000 leak — and underpricing there costs you more than losing the deal.

**Question 3 scored 3 or 4 but everything else is low.** They don't need a website, they need automation. Sell the standalone AI readiness assessment at $2,500, then scope from what it finds. Do not force them into a web package to fit the menu.

## Presenting the recommendation

Name one tier. Not two, not a menu.

> "Based on what you've described, Professional is the right fit. Here's why: you've got four people losing about ten hours a week to manual intake, and you're running a CRM that doesn't talk to your site. Essential would fix the website but leave that ten hours exactly where it is. Professional puts two automations in place that take it off your team entirely."

Then send the one-tier PDF within 24 hours. If they ask what else exists, show the tier directly above — never below. Showing the tier below is how you talk yourself into a smaller deal.

### If they're between two tiers

Recommend the higher one and name the specific thing that justifies it. If they push back, the downgrade is their decision, not your opening offer. Starting low and upselling later is much harder than starting right and holding.

---

## Qualifying out

Some calls should end without a proposal. Recognizing them early is a skill worth more than a close.

- **"Can you just fix a couple of things on my current site?"** — that's hourly work at $150/hr, not a package. Quote it as such or refer it out.
- **No budget conversation at all by minute 12.** Ask directly: "Do you have a budget range in mind?" If the answer is "we want to see proposals first," they're collecting quotes to beat someone down on price.
- **They want AI but won't give system access.** You'll be blamed for an integration you weren't allowed to build.
- **Two or more agencies fired in eighteen months.** You'll be the third.
- **They negotiate before scope is defined.** The frame is already wrong and pricing won't fix it.
- **The ask is entirely outside Platforms and AI Consulting.** Refer it. A good referral comes back.

## Questions you will get, and the answers

**"Why is this more than the quote I got for $1,200?"**

> "It probably is more. That quote is almost certainly a template with your logo on it, which is a real option — that's our Starter tier at $1,500. The difference at Essential is that it's built from scratch to load in under two seconds and rank in local search, and it's maintained every month afterward. If a template is genuinely what you need, I'll build you one and I won't try to talk you up."

**"Can I get Professional without the monthly?"**

No, and hold this line. The automations require monitoring — APIs change, models get deprecated, and an unmaintained automation fails silently, which is worse than not having it. If they only want a build, that's Essential plus workflow add-ons at $2,500 each, and the failure risk is theirs in writing.

**"What if I want to cancel?"**

> "Thirty days' notice after the initial term, and you keep everything — code, content, domain, data, all of it in accounts already in your name. I've deliberately made it easy to leave. It keeps me honest about earning the monthly."

**"Can you do it cheaper?"**

Never cut a tier price. Move them down a tier, or reduce scope at the same rate. A discounted Essential teaches the client that your prices are opening offers, and every renewal after that becomes a negotiation.
