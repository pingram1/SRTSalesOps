## 1. Executive Summary

You told us that the phone rings while your team is on site, and nobody picks it up. That the website "looks fine on my laptop" but takes eight seconds to load on the phone most of your buyers are actually using. And that Marisa spends most of Friday retyping lead information from her notepad into HubSpot — work she described, accurately, as something a computer should be doing.

None of this is a technology problem. It is three separate leaks in the path between someone deciding they want a custom home and you finding out about it.

Three things are costing you money right now:

1. **Missed inbound calls** — you estimate three qualified leads per month reach voicemail and never call back. At your close rate and your margins, that's $450,000 in gross profit per year that goes to whoever answers on the second ring.
2. **An eight-second mobile site** — 53% of mobile visitors leave a page that takes longer than three seconds. Most people searching "custom home builder Austin" on their phone never see your portfolio at all.
3. **15 hours a week of manual lead entry** — roughly $20,000 a year of Marisa's time spent on data transfer, and a lag between a lead arriving and anyone acting on it.

Together these represent an estimated **$39,180 per month** in revenue you are not capturing and labor you are paying for twice. That figure is derived entirely from numbers you gave us on March 11 — the arithmetic is shown in section 3 so you can check it.

## 2. The Digital Transformation Plan

Two systems, built to work as one. The first makes sure people looking for a builder like you actually find you and stay long enough to see your work. The second makes sure that when they call, someone always answers — and that what they say ends up in HubSpot without anyone retyping it.

### A website built to load in under two seconds

**What it is.** A rebuild of smithcocustomhomes.com from scratch, hand-coded rather than assembled from a theme. Your portfolio photography stays at full quality but loads progressively, so the page is usable before the images finish. The site is structured so Google can tell what you build, where you build it, and what a project costs — which is what determines whether you appear in local search.

**What changes for you.** Someone in Westlake searching "custom home builder near me" on their phone at 9pm finds Smith & Co. before they find the two national franchises currently ranking above you. The page loads before they lose patience. Your Barton Creek build is the first thing they see.

**How we'll know it worked.** Mobile load time under 2 seconds, down from 8.1. Mobile bounce rate down from 71%. Organic local search impressions tracked monthly against a March baseline.

### An AI receptionist that answers every call

**What it is.** A voice system that picks up on the first ring, any hour, when your team can't. It sounds like a person, and it is briefed on your business — it knows you build custom, not spec; that you work in Travis and Hays counties; that your builds start around $1.4M. It asks the four qualifying questions Marisa currently asks, and it never asks them impatiently.

**What changes for you.** No lead reaches voicemail. A qualified caller at 7:30pm on a Saturday gets a real conversation, gets their questions answered, and books a consultation on your calendar. By the time you see it Monday, the lead is already in HubSpot with a full transcript, scored, and assigned. Unqualified callers are handled politely and never reach your calendar at all.

**How we'll know it worked.** Call answer rate at 100%, up from an estimated 62%. Qualified leads reaching HubSpot within 60 seconds of the call ending. Monthly review of every transcript the system scored as qualified, so you can tell us where it got it wrong.

### HubSpot sync that removes the retyping

**What it is.** Every lead — from the website, the phone, or a referral form — lands in HubSpot as a complete record. Contact details, budget range, lot status, timeline, and the full call transcript. No copying, no notepad, no Friday afternoon.

**What changes for you.** Marisa gets roughly 15 hours a week back. Based on your conversation with her, that time goes to client communication during active builds, which is the thing you said actually drives referrals.

**How we'll know it worked.** Manual entry hours at zero, down from 60 per month. Lead-to-first-contact time measured weekly.

## 3. The ROI Projection

Every number below comes from the discovery call on March 11. Where we had to estimate, we chose the conservative figure and marked it.

### What's being lost today

**Missed inbound leads**

```
 3 leads recovered per month
  × 12 months
  × 25% close rate              ← your number, not an industry average
  × $50,000 gross profit per job ← gross profit, not contract value
  = $450,000 per year
```

Two deliberately conservative choices here. We used your 25% close rate rather than the 31% the NAHB reports for custom builders in your price band. And we used $50,000 gross profit rather than your $1.4M average contract value — you keep roughly 3.6% of contract as profit on these builds, and that is the only number worth putting in a business case. This is the defensible floor, not the ceiling.

**Manual lead entry**

```
 60 hours per month
  × 12 months
  × $28 fully loaded hourly cost ← $44k salary ÷ 2,080 hrs × 1.3 for tax, benefits, overhead
  = $20,160 per year
```

We have not counted the second-order value of that time — faster follow-up, better client communication during builds, the referrals you said those conversations generate. It is real, but we cannot measure it, so it is not in the model.

### Total annual opportunity

| Stream | Annual value |
|---|---|
| Missed inbound leads | $450,000 |
| Manual lead entry | $20,160 |
| **Total** | **$470,160** |

**This works out to $39,180 for every month the current setup stays in place.**

That is the number this proposal is measured against. Not our fee — the cost of the alternative.

## Objection handling — Smith & Co. specifics

### If Dale says the price is too high

**Reframe first.**

> "I understand. Let me put it next to the other number. Staying as you are costs about **$39,180 a month** — that's your figure, from your close rate and your margins. Professional runs **$3,200 a month**. So the real question isn't whether $3,200 is a lot of money. It's whether you'd rather keep paying $39,180 for nothing."

Then stop talking.

**Isolate.**

> "Can I ask which of these it actually is? Is it that the money isn't there this quarter, or that you're not convinced we'll hit those numbers? Those are completely different problems and I'd rather solve the right one."

- **Budget** — 40/30/30 across milestones, or move to Essential and add the receptionist in Q3.
- **Belief** — go back to section 3 and rebuild it live with his numbers. He is a builder; he will respect being shown the framing.
- **Timing** — "What would need to be true by June?" Then: three months of delay is $117,540.

**Scope, never discount.**

> "I'm not going to drop the price, and here's why: I built it from your numbers. If I knocked six thousand off it now, you'd be right to wonder what the first number was based on. What I can change is what we build."

### If he wants the website only

> "Yes. That's Essential, and it's a real option. Worth knowing what it leaves behind: the site fixes about $211,000 of the $470,000 we modeled. The other half is the phone — those three calls a month keep going to voicemail. Happy to start with Essential and add the receptionist in Q3. I'd just want you to pick it knowing the larger half is still on the table."

### If he says the AI receptionist will sound like a robot to luxury buyers

The one objection most likely to actually kill this deal. Do not argue it — demo it.

> "That's the right concern, and it's the one I'd have. Let me not answer it. Give me your cell — I'll have it call you tomorrow at 10. Ask it something a real buyer would ask. If it sounds like a robot, we take it out of the proposal and rebuild the numbers around the website."

Then follow through. This objection is unwinnable by argument and trivially winnable by demonstration.

### If he says his nephew can build the website

> "He might do a good job of it. The part that's hard to hand to family is section four — someone showing you every month whether it's actually earning, and being on the hook when it isn't. That's uncomfortable to ask a nephew for. It's the whole reason we're worth paying."

### Reminders

- Present descending: Elite, Professional, Essential.
- Say nothing in the four seconds after naming a price.
- Do not say "it's an investment, not a cost." The table already said it.
- If he negotiates before section 3 has landed, stop and go back to section 3. The frame is wrong and pricing will not fix it.
